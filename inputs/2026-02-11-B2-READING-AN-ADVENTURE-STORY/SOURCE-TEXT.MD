> ### \*\*About Aesop's Fables\*\*

> 

> 

> By tradition, Aesop was a slave in Ancient Greece who was born around 620 BC and was set free because of his storytelling ability.

> Over the years, a large number of stories have been attributed to Aesop. This collection of stories is known as \*Aesop's Fables\*. Some of the most famous ones are \*The Tortoise and the Hare\*, \*The Boy who Cried Wolf\*, \*The Goose that Laid the Golden Eggs\*, and \*The Miser and his Gold\*.

> The fables are popular around the world because they are short, contain animals or vivid characters, and give clear moral guidance. In recent times, they have been adapted into cartoons, musicals, and modern stories.

\*\*Literary strategy: Analyzing the moral of a story\*\*



> The moral of a story is a lesson about life that the author expresses through the events of the story. It is usually shown through the experience of the main character. The aim of the moral is to encourage readers to reflect on and improve their own behavior. For example, the moral of \*The Tortoise and the Hare\* is "those who are too confident, and don't respect their opponent or the task that they have to do, often fail."
