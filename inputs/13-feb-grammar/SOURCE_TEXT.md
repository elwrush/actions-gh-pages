# Source Text: Grammar (used to vs would) - Combined

## Main Aim
By the end of the lesson, learners will be better able to describe past habits and states using **used to** and **would** accurately in spoken and written contexts.

## Worksheet Mission
In your B1 Preliminary (PET) exam, you often have to describe past experiences and habits in the Speaking and Writing sections. Mastering the difference between 'used to' and 'would' will make your descriptions much more natural and precise.

*   🎯 *Identify* the difference between past states and past actions.
*   📝 *Repair* sentences by choosing the most precise grammatical structure.
*   🗣️ *Produce* accurate descriptions of your own childhood habits.

## Stage 1: Lead-in (5 Min) - Lesson Plan Content
**Aim**: To engage Ss and activate schemata about past habits.

*   **Hook:** T writes "When I was 10, I..." on the board.
*   T gives a personal example (e.g., "I used to play hide and seek").
*   **Elicit:** Ss brainstorm 3 things they did as children that they don't do now.
*   **Interaction:** Ss discuss in pairs.

## Task 1: Complete the Dialogue (Worksheet Exercise)
Complete the dialogue with the correct form of *used to* and short answers.

*Amy:* Have you ever lived abroad? 
*Ben:* Yes, I have. I lived in China for a few years. 
*Amy:* (1) ________ (you / go) to school in China? 
*Ben:* Yes, I (2) ________. 
*Amy:* Wow, that's really cool. Why were you there? 
*Ben:* My mother (3) ________ (work) for a company that had an office in Shanghai. 
*Amy:* Shanghai? That's so interesting. 
*Ben:* But you (4) ________ (live) abroad, too, right? Was it Germany? 
*Amy:* No, we didn't live there. But we (5) ________ (visit) my grandparents there every summer.

## Stage 3: Teach (10 Min) - Lesson Plan Content
**Aim**: To clarify meaning, form, and pronunciation of target language.

*   **Feedback:** T reviews answers on the board for Ex 5 & 7.
*   **Meaning (CCQ):** "Do we still do these things now?" (No). "Can we use 'would' for living somewhere?" (No, only for actions).
*   **Form:** Clarify that *used to* works for BOTH states and habits. *Would* works ONLY for repeated actions/habits in the past.
*   **Pronunciation:** Model and drill the pronunciation of "used to" /juːst tʊ/ (not "yooz-d"). Chorally and individually.

## Task 2: The Replacement Challenge (Worksheet Exercise)
Read the text about Two Super-Smart Kids. Decide if the numbered *used to* phrases can be replaced with *would* or *wouldn't*. If possible, write *would* or *wouldn't*. If not possible (because it is a state verb), write a dash (---).

*Taylor Wilson* (1) used to dream of being a scientist. As a young child, he (2) used to read books about kids who liked science - books like Ken Silverstein's _The Radioactive Boy Scout_. He became fascinated by radiation. So, he (3) didn't use to spend his weekends being lazy, like some of his friends. Instead, he (4) used to go around his hometown on the weekends, searching for natural radioactivity. He (5) used to live in Arkansas, in the US, but his family moved to Nevada so he could attend a school for gifted students. At the age of 14, he built a nuclear reactor!

When she was a child, *Brittany Wenger's* parents (6) used to tell her that she was going to be great one day. At school, she (7) used to spend a lot of time in the computer lab, learning to code. But she (8) didn't use to ignore her other subjects - she (9) used to get good grades in everything. Brittany (10) used to live with her family in Ohio, but now she studies computer science at Duke University.

## Task 3: Sentence Repair (Worksheet Exercise)
Complete the sentences with *used to / didn't use to* or *would / wouldn't*. For some sentences, both forms are possible.

1. In the summer, the kids in the neighborhood ________ often stay up late playing outside.
2. The students at my new school ________ be strangers, but now they're friends.
3. Before I got a bike, I ________ cycle to school. I walked every day.
4. My dad ________ work as a lawyer, but then he retrained to become a teacher.
5. We ________ live in a house, we lived in an apartment.

## Task 4: My Life, Then and Now (Worksheet Exercise)
Think about a major change in your life (e.g., starting a new school, moving house, getting a new hobby). Write three sentences comparing your habits and states from before the change to now, using *used to* and *would*.

Share your sentences with a partner or small group!
