Let's learn some words

5 key words from the audio

Pre-teach these 5 words before students listen. They appear in the audio script and removing this barrier will improve comprehension significantly.
Elicit the meaning before revealing. Ask: 'If you cannot leave a place, you are...?' Drill the /æ/ vowel sound.

trapped

Phoneme /træpt/
Context "The coach led 12 boys into a cave, where they became trapped for two weeks."
Point to the background image. 'What happened here? The place is completely filled with water — it's flooded.' Drill the /ʌ/ sound.

flooded

Phoneme /ˈflʌd.ɪd/
Context "After the rain, the cave quickly became flooded with water."
Concept check: 'Is being rescued good or bad?' (Good — you are saved from danger). Note the passive form: 'were rescued'.

rescued

Phoneme /ˈres.kjuːd/
Context "After 17 days, all 13 people were rescued safely by expert divers."
This is the key opinion word. 'Reckless = you do something dangerous without caring about the risk.' Ask: 'Is reckless a positive or negative word?' (Negative, judgmental).

reckless

Phoneme /ˈrek.ləs/
Context "Some people said the coach was reckless because he didn't think about the danger."
IMPORTANT: This word is a false clue in the T/F task (Q3 is FALSE). The boys were scared but NOT panicking. Draw this contrast now to prepare learners.

panicked

Phoneme /ˈpæn.ɪkt/
Context "The boys stayed calm — they had NOT panicked when rescuers found them."

Listen Closely

Audio 4.02 — The Full Newscast

Now students need precision. They should read the 6 sentences BEFORE listening to activate their prediction skills.

STRATEGY

True or False

Before: Read all 6 statements. Predict which words you will hear.
During: Listen for words that match or contradict each statement.
TRUE: The statement matches the audio exactly. Even one wrong detail = FALSE.
FALSE: One detail is wrong, exaggerated, or missing from the audio.
Give students 60 seconds to read the statements before playing the audio. Model strategy on item 1 if needed.

Hero, Villain, or Something Else?

Post-Task Discussion

Transition to the discussion phase. Tell students there is no single right answer — this is about forming and defending an opinion using evidence.

STRATEGY

Give an Opinion

State your position:
"I think he is a hero / villain..."
Give a reason:
"Because he kept the boys calm for 9 days."
Add evidence:
"Guest 2 said he showed 'incredibly strong leadership.'"
Acknowledge the other side:
"He made a mistake — but he also saved them."
Model one full opinion first: 'I think he is something else, because...' Then give students the timer to discuss in pairs.
TASK 3

Coach Ekapol Chanthawong — Discussion

Discussion Questions
Was the coach a hero, a villain, or something else?
Can someone make a bad decision but still be a good person?
Use evidence from the audio to support your opinion.
05:00
5-minute pair/group discussion. Listen to student arguments. Final vote: hands up for hero / villain / something else. Ask students to justify their position with evidence from the audio.

LANGUAGE

Expressing Opinions — From the Audio

Giving an opinion "I think that shows strong leadership."
Expressing uncertainty "I'm not sure we should judge him."
Hedging a claim "He probably shouldn't have led them there."
Conceding a point "OK, so the coach caused the problem — yes, but..."
Draw students' attention to how native speakers express opinions carefully. These are B1-level opinion hedges. Have students practise one each using the discussion topic.
